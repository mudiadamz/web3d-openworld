Keys

Moving
WASD(in Orbit) slide the rig across the ground, so you can leave the meadow without losing the pivot. Clicking the map is the quicker way to get anywhere
QEdown and up (in Orbit)
shiftsprint. There is one move speed and it is not adjustable — it is set by DAY_LENGTH, because a short day is a fast one
draglook around — orbits the pivot in Orbit, circles the person in Follow
scrollzoom in Orbit · how far back you stand in Follow
Views Orbit
Cthe other view — Orbit is a rig you point at a place, Follow is a person you go with. Fly and Walk are gone: what they were for is what clicking the map does, in one gesture
Ffollow somebody — again for somebody else. Their energy is on the caption, because it is what decides what they take on
Rsomewhere else on the island — drops you into Orbit over a random spot, and again for another one. Dry land and not a cliff, so there is always something under you
click(in Follow) go there — click the ground and the person you are behind walks to it, on their own, with a ring on the spot. Click again and they turn round. Nothing else steers them while you have them — not dusk, not a job, not a tiger — but they still tire, still get hungry, and can still be caught
WASD(in Follow) walk them yourself — W is the way the camera looks. Hold shift to run: it is a jog and it is charged for like one, it slows with a full basket, and it gives out when there is nothing left. Clicking the ground still sends them somewhere
E(in Follow) do what is in front of them — pick fruit, forage at a berry thicket, cut wood at a tree (the logs go on the band's stack by the granaries when put away, and a band on a coast builds its raft out of that stack), take the band's raft out from its dock and fish from it out on deep water (there is no fishing from the bank, and E back at the dock ties it up), dig a quarry, tend the fire, or throw a spear at the nearest animal (a tiger first, if one is in reach) — a ring round them shows how far a spear goes, green once something is inside it, and what they bring down lies where it fell until E picks it up — and inside the ring round the granaries, put away what they are carrying. The prompt at the bottom says which, a ring on the ground marks each thing within a short walk, and the one E would act on is green. They are still yours when it is done
Z(in Follow) take cover — up the tree they are at, where a tiger cannot reach them, or down low anywhere else, where it has to nearly walk onto them. Down low, WASD crawls: slowly, and nothing grazing notices them — a crawl is how to get within a throw, and a throw from down low is surer. Z again to come down or stand, or shift to get up and run. A tiger in sight is shown at the top, with which way and how far
G(in Follow) put a handful down in front of them — ten berries, five fruit, a fish, a stone, an animal — and shift+G for all of it. It stays on the ground with a ring round it, and E picks it up again; food left lying spoils in a few days. A load weighs what it is — the fuller, the slower — and past what they can carry they cannot walk, though they can still do whatever is in reach
X(in Follow) sit down to rest, or get up. Resting wins some of the life bar back — faster at home by their own fire, where an illness passes quicker too — but never all of it: only food does that
N(in Follow) eat: out of the band's store at home, out of the basket anywhere else. A meal gives back more of the life bar than resting can. The bar under the basket is their life, out of a hundred: walking, far more running, working, carrying, and the cold away from the camp at night or in winter all wear it down, and near empty they slow and cannot run
Q(in Follow) let go, and they pick up their own life where they stand
icons(in Follow) a row across the bottom tells them what to do rather than where to go — find food, hunt, knap, tend the fire, rest, walk to the next band. One instruction, taken up once: afterwards they are choosing for themselves again
On screen
Hthe world panel, minimised to its icon rather than hidden — the icon stays where it is, so the way back is the thing you just used
shift+Fback to whoever you were watching before — F picks at random, so this is the way back to somebody you did not choose in the first place. Out of Follow it returns you to them rather than to a stranger
V(in Follow) back over their shoulder — the angle and the distance F left you at, and the camera keeps that station as they walk instead of holding a compass bearing while they turn away. Dragging to look around lets go of their shoulder; V takes it again
Mthe map: off, a corner map, the whole window, and round again. Full page has a frame, the band codes, the paths drawn as roads, a scale, and buttons to zoom and to put it back in the corner; the wheel zooms it too. Zoomed in it follows you until you drag it, and then it stays where you put it — zoom back out to hand it back. A click still travels; what tells the two apart is how far the pointer moved. Clicking a band's dot goes to their camp and opens their card — the cursor turns to a pointer over one. shift+M goes back the other way. Click it to travel there
[]slower and faster — the clock, and everything living on it. Once everybody is in, the night is run through rather than watched and takes about a second; NIGHT_SKIP_RATE is how much of each frame is spent doing it
Bhide one more layer — press until something stops looking wrong, and the last name shown is the culprit. shift+B puts it all back
Tthe band you are watching — who they are, their ages, how many children, what each of them has carried home. Click a name to follow them, which is how you reach somebody who is indoors or over a hill
Lthe whole chronicle — every line from every world, searchable. It opens on what is worth telling: what a band worked out, who broke away and who went with them, who took somebody in, the days the store ran out. The button beside it gives back every kill and birth as well
